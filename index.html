Before you start

This tool works when the conversation happens first. It's a scaffold, not a jailer — the goal is for kids to see their time so they can shape it, not to enforce a parent-imposed grid.

Talk about it together

Before adding blocks, agree as a family on the shape of the week:

  • What's non-negotiable, and why? (School, sleep, meals.)
  • What's flexible? (Screens, free play, order of homework.)
  • What does everyone need — the kids and the adults?

The clock ends you, not the task

When a block's timer goes off, you're done — even mid-job. You don't have to finish it, you have to spend the time. If you stop in the middle, the supplies still get put away.

Two things aren't on the clock, because they aren't chores

  • When you leave a room, your dishes and your trash come with you.
  • Clean up what you see on the way past, whoever it belongs to.

No counting, nobody keeping score on that second part. If everybody does it, it balances out.

Give it two weeks

New routines take a couple of weeks to feel normal. Plan to sit down at day 14 and adjust together. That check-in matters more than any single day.

Repair, don't punish

Missed reading, over-screened afternoons — these are data, not verdicts. This app will show what happened; the conversation with the kid is where meaning gets made.

Two views, same data

Parent view shows time budgets and observations. Kid view hides those so kids can just design their day. Toggle at any time in the top right.
