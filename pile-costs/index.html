WHERE IT GOES

What those hours actually are

The number above is not one big job. It is a few hundred small ones that come back.

Kind of workWhat is in itHours a year

Hygiene and getting dressed are not counted here. Neither is cooking. This is only the work of keeping the house itself running.

And the goal is not a house that stays clean. It is one that comes back fast after a bad week. Nothing on this list gets done every single time. What decides whether the house recovers is how much of it gets handled on the way past, before it turns into a pile that needs a whole afternoon.

THE ASK

Thirty minutes

Thirty minutes a day, on the days the kids are around. They pick what it goes to. Their room, their laundry, their backpack, or anything off the house list. Their own stuff counts. When the timer goes off they're done, even in the middle of something. The clock ends you, not the task. No points, no charts, no supervision.

Two things aren't on the clock, because they aren't chores. When you leave a room, your dishes and your trash come with you. And clean up what you see on the way past, whoever it belongs to. That isn't work anybody assigns you — it's what using a shared space costs, the same way you put a controller back before you use the TV again.

No counting, nobody keeping score on that second part. Some days one person handles more of the other's stuff. Some days it flips. If everybody does it, it balances. Nobody can vacuum just their own dust or wipe down half a mirror.

The other half of the deal: everything that isn't in the kids' thirty minutes is on the adult(s). That's the part of this page that's really for them.
